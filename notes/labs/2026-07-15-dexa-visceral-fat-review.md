## Working Interpretation

This VAT reading does not stand alone — it lines up with the 2026-06-29 blood work in [[2026-06-29-lab-review]]:

- A1c 5.8% (prediabetes range)
- Triglycerides 363 mg/dL (high)
- HDL 37 mg/dL (low)
- ALT 51 IU/L (mildly elevated)

That panel's working interpretation was insulin resistance, possibly with early metabolic fatty liver. Elevated visceral fat is mechanistically consistent with that picture — VAT drives insulin resistance more directly than subcutaneous fat, and the two findings reinforce each other rather than being independent data points. This raises the priority of the metabolic panel's follow-up, not just the body-comp goal.

## Practical Focus

Same direction as the 2026-06-29 panel's practical focus, now with a second data point pointing the same way:

- Cut added sugar, sugary drinks, refined carbs — the single biggest food lever, no tracking system required.
- Minimize alcohol — liver deprioritizes fat oxidation to process alcohol first.
- Add zone-2 + short interval cardio on top of existing lifting (150-300 min/week combined) — the mechanism most directly tied to VAT reduction in the cited research, independent of the metabolic-panel remediation.
- Protect sleep (10pm lights-out) — poor sleep raises cortisol, and visceral fat carries more cortisol receptors than subcutaneous fat.
- 10-minute walk after the biggest meal — cheap, no-tracking habit that blunts the post-meal glucose spike feeding both the VAT and A1c pictures.

## Expected Timeline

- Visceral fat: meaningful DEXA-visible change over ~2-3 months of consistent execution, per the cited cycling study.
- Metabolic panel (A1c, triglycerides, HDL, ALT): per the 2026-06-29 review, triglycerides can respond within weeks, liver markers within 2-4 months, A1c reflects ~3 months of average glucose.
