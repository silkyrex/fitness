## Working Interpretation

The main pattern is metabolic:

- A1c in the prediabetes range
- High fasting triglycerides
- Low HDL
- Mild ALT elevation

Together, these findings are consistent with insulin resistance. Metabolic fatty liver is plausible, but it is not established by this blood test. Other explanations for ALT elevation include alcohol, medication or supplements, recent intense exercise, temporary illness, and viral hepatitis.

Estimated FIB-4 is approximately 0.52. This is below the 1.3 threshold used to identify higher concern for advanced liver fibrosis. It does not rule out early liver fat or early fibrosis.

There is no laboratory evidence here of advanced liver dysfunction. AST, bilirubin, albumin, and platelets are normal.

## Follow-Up

At the next clinician visit:

1. Correct the fasting-status history.
2. Confirm that follow-up testing includes a fasting lipid panel, A1c, and liver enzymes.
3. Ask whether triglycerides should be repeated sooner than four months.
4. Review alcohol intake, medications, supplements, weight or waist changes, blood pressure, exercise, and family cardiovascular history.
5. Ask whether hepatitis B and C screening is complete.
6. If ALT remains elevated, discuss liver imaging such as ultrasound or FibroScan.

## Practical Focus

- Remove or sharply reduce sugary drinks, sweets, and refined carbohydrates.
- Minimize alcohol while the liver finding is being evaluated.
- Exercise consistently, combining cardio and resistance work.
- If excess weight is present, pursue gradual weight loss rather than crash dieting.
- Do not use liver-detox products or over-the-counter fish oil as a substitute for medical follow-up.

## Expected Timeline

- Triglycerides can respond within weeks.
- Liver fat and ALT may improve within two to four months.
- A1c reflects roughly three months of average glucose.
- Fibrosis, if present, generally changes over years rather than weeks.
